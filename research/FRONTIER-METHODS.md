## What the agents actually built and established

- **g1-2000001 (six lines).** The saved proof packs two useful consequences into one distribution: from `~S`, Addition introduces a structured disjunct `(~S . ~~R)`, then Distribution yields both an idempotent `~S v ~S` component and a `~S v ~~R` component for De Morgan and double-negation steps. The archive includes executable Python search/check programs and result logs. Tool receipts show invocations of `independent_short_search.py`, `independent_chain_multiadd.py`, `independent_r_intro.py`, `independent_ip_check.py`, `independent_exact_inverse.py`, and `independent_restricted_inverse.py`; these are real implemented investigations, not just a method named in prose. One short-search invocation failed with exit 127. The unrestricted exact-inverse log ends `Killed`, while its enclosing shell receipt has exit 0; the receipt does not authenticate the cause of termination. A restricted inverse search reports no five-line chain in its specified rule class, and a direct indirect-proof enumeration reports no contradiction in its bounded intermediate space. Neither covers every allowed proof. The journal contrasts a seven-line construction, but owner candidate receipts show the first accepted proof at six lines and subsequent six-line duplicates, **not** a separately accepted seven-line incumbent. The observed result is a valid six-line proof, 7 lines below par, with no global optimality certificate.
- **g2-2100023 (five lines).** The saved `tools/make_proof.py` writes the proof JSON, and tool receipts show two successful invocations. The method extracts `~P` and `S` by Simplification, uses Addition to append the theorem's large conditional as an arbitrary disjunct of `~P`, then conjoins the pieces. The third premise is unused in the final proof. A delegate receipt supports that a four-line alternative was requested for review; there is no saved executable exhaustive four-line search or accepted four-line candidate. The owner receipts show one invalid candidate, then an accepted five-line proof and five-line duplicate/non-improvement receipts. The observed result is a valid five-line proof, 18 lines below par.
- **g3-2200607 (17 lines).** The saved `tools/make_candidateA.py` builds a candidate proof. Tool receipts show its first invocation failing, followed by successful invocations. Its saved `tools/candidateA.json` has the same byte hash as the final graded proof. The construction normalizes a disjunction from premise 2 once and reuses it, derives an immediate `~~S > ~~S` identity by conditional proof instead of simplifying the repeated-S premise 3, then builds the target conjunctions and outer conditional. The journal describes additional Distribution/Exportation and rearrangement checks, but no saved executable general search establishes exhaustive exploration of those routes. The owner receipts show one accepted 17-line proof and 14 17-line duplicates/non-improvements. The observed result is a valid 17-line proof, 8 lines below par.

The `g1` bounded negative checks concern specified search spaces and rule restrictions; the `g2` and `g3` non-improvement notes concern attempted local reviews. None is a lower bound for the unrestricted benchmark. The reductions from par are observed score improvements only. They do not identify the causal contribution of tool access, and no later cumulative inheritance is included here.

## A subsequent cumulative improvement: 10 to 9 lines

The separate cumulative run `548e0ec9-1dea-4ba6-b444-fda190043605` on `g1-2000013` inherited the 10-line proof from fresh run `71ed4f97-641e-4d51-9f6b-0d5fd1613841` and received an additional 900-second allowance. It produced an accepted nine-line proof. The [fresh proof](../publication/data/proofs/71ed4f97-641e-4d51-9f6b-0d5fd1613841/g1-2000013.json) and [cumulative proof](../publication/data/proofs/548e0ec9-1dea-4ba6-b444-fda190043605/g1-2000013.json) show the concrete change.

The fresh proof constructed `~(~~Q . ~Q)` through Addition, Double Negation and De Morgan: three derived lines after extracting `~Q`. The continuation instead assumes `~~Q . ~Q`, which is already a contradiction, and discharges that assumption immediately with `IP 2-2`. The assumption and closing line both count, so this two-line route saves exactly one line. The remainder of the construction is the same pattern. Immediate discharge of a contradictory assumption is accepted by the stated rule semantics; this is an observed proof improvement, not an optimality claim or an equal-resource comparison.
